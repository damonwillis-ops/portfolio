Point of View

Most internal platforms fail because nobody owns the middle.

Engineering builds what's on the ticket. Operations asks for what they need today. And somewhere in between, the roadmap turns into a list of whoever shouted loudest last week. The platforms that actually get adopted are the ones where someone owns that gap: filtering requests, scoping work properly, saying no when it doesn't fit, and explaining why in a way that doesn't make people want to go around you.

That's the work I do. The platforms below exist because I identified the problem, wrote the specs, built them, shipped them, measured whether they worked, and kept iterating. The enterprise adoption work happened because I owned the business case, the stakeholder alignment, and the ongoing prioritization. Not one or the other. Both.

Design Validation & Partner Performance Platform
Award-Winning

Third-party design firms were submitting work with no way to self-check quality before it hit our team. I built a platform to fix that. It's a drag-and-drop tool where 24 external partner firms validate their submissions against a 12-table schema before anything reaches us. If something fails, they get an error report and fix it themselves.

I also built partner performance analytics on top of it: error rates, submission patterns, and quality trends per firm. First time anyone at Walmart had visibility into designer performance at this level. Won the Walmart Making the Difference Award in 2026.

Enterprise Platform Adoption — B2B SaaS, 4,600+ Locations
Roadmap Owner

Walmart had no structured record of its physical security infrastructure across 4,600+ locations. Design data lived in static files, nothing carried forward between project phases, and leadership was making capital decisions on incomplete information.

I identified the problem, quantified the cost (significant quarterly rework and data loss), ran the competitive evaluation, and wrote the Director-grade business case with a self-funding model embedded in existing budgets and a seven-figure annual return. Got it through SSP and Global Tech governance and secured SVP sponsorship. Then I owned the ongoing relationship between five business functions and the platform team, managing what got built, what waited, and why.

AI Design Assurance Platform
Production

Camera designs were getting signed off without anyone checking whether they met the AI computer-vision requirements. Problems only showed up after installation, which meant truck rolls and rework. I built a platform to catch those problems before install: it ingests design exports with geocoordinate precision, validates each camera placement against codified requirements, and produces pass/fail design reviews that gate sign-off.

It also automates device data population that used to be manual entry across thousands of records. I owned it from problem identification through requirements, build, and ongoing iteration.

Intelligence Engine & Decision System
Production
